# Expectations

## Employees — Fast Help When It Matters

Employees who trigger an alert expect a qualified person to arrive within
minutes, not after a chain of phone calls. They expect the system to be simple
enough to use under stress — one action, not a form to fill out. They also
expect that the system won't be used to surveil them.

Interested party: employees (alerters and responders).
Type: desired.
Gives rise to: risk-adoption, risk-works-council.
Fulfilled by: product-alert-service.

## Safety Officers — Compliance and Audit Trail

Safety officers and occupational health teams need to demonstrate that the
organization has a systematic alerting capability. They expect the system to
produce records of alert issuance, response times, and outcomes — not for
surveillance, but for compliance with workplace safety regulations (ArbSchG,
DGUV) and internal safety audits.

Interested party: internal stakeholders (safety and compliance).
Type: mandatory (regulatory — ArbSchG, DGUV Vorschrift 1).
Gives rise to: opportunity-internal-mandate.
Fulfilled by: product-alert-service.

## Works Council — Privacy Safeguards

The works council expects that any location-aware system includes clear
boundaries: location data used only for active alert situations, no
continuous tracking, no performance monitoring derived from location data,
transparent data retention and deletion policies, and genuinely voluntary
participation.

Interested party: works council (Betriebsrat).
Type: mandatory (co-determination under BetrVG §87).
Gives rise to: risk-works-council.
Fulfilled by: capability-location-routing.

## Management — Cost-Effective Alternative

Management sponsors expect a solution that costs significantly less per site
than the existing enterprise notification system while covering the fast,
local alerting use case. They expect a company-wide rollout within 12 months,
not a multi-year project.

Interested party: internal stakeholders (management sponsors).
Type: desired.
Gives rise to: opportunity-cost-effective-alerting, risk-rollout-timeline.
Fulfilled by: product-alert-service.

## IT Operations — Fit Into Existing Infrastructure

The IT department expects the system to work with existing corporate
infrastructure rather than requiring its own parallel stack. A system that
can't integrate will face adoption resistance from IT operations regardless
of its merits.

Interested party: internal stakeholders (IT operations).
Type: desired.
Gives rise to: risk-adoption.
Fulfilled by: capability-platform-integration.

## Sensor and Building Systems — Easy Integration

Existing IoT and building management systems need to connect to the alerting
service without requiring changes on their side. Each sensor type works
differently; the alerting service must accommodate that variety.

Interested party: partners (IoT/BMS system operators).
Type: desired.
Gives rise to: opportunity-sensor-bridge.
Fulfilled by: product-sensor-bridge.
