# Signals

## Political Pressure After Workplace Incidents

Several high-profile workplace incidents in the DACH transportation sector have
drawn media and political scrutiny. In each case, the response time from incident
to first aid was criticized — not because responders were slow, but because they
weren't notified fast enough. Political and public pressure is pushing large
employers toward demonstrable, systematic alerting capabilities.

Source: external (political, media).
Gives rise to: opportunity-internal-mandate, risk-reputational.

## Existing Enterprise Notification Systems Are Overbuilt for Local Alerting

The organization currently uses an enterprise notification system designed for
crisis management: mass notification, escalation workflows, situation rooms.
It works for organization-wide alerts but is too expensive per seat, too slow
to customize, and fundamentally not designed for "someone nearby needs help
right now, who is closest?"

Source: internal (operational gap).
Gives rise to: opportunity-cost-effective-alerting, risk-incumbent-response.

## Employees Are Reachable on Personal Devices

Virtually all employees carry a personal device capable of receiving
notifications. Corporate device management is in place. This makes a
device-based alerting channel feasible without requiring dedicated hardware
at every workstation — though physical alert triggers remain important for
hands-busy environments.

Source: internal (technology readiness).
Gives rise to: opportunity-device-based-alerting.

## IoT Sensor Networks Expanding Across Sites

Industrial sites and buildings within the organization are increasingly equipped
with sensors — gas detectors, environmental monitoring, fire detection, access
control. These sensors generate events but most lack a direct path to human
alerting. The data exists; the bridge to "notify the nearest person" does not.

Source: internal (technology readiness).
Gives rise to: opportunity-sensor-bridge.

## Positioning Technology Is Mature Enough for Proximity Routing

Indoor positioning and asset tracking have reached a maturity level where
it is feasible to answer "who is near this incident?" at reasonable cost.
This is the technical prerequisite for routing alerts by proximity rather
than broadcasting to lists.

Source: external (technology).
Gives rise to: opportunity-location-routing.

## Works Council Sensitivity to Employee Tracking

Employee location tracking is a highly sensitive topic under German labor law.
Works councils (Betriebsräte) have co-determination rights on any system that
monitors employee behavior or location. Any location-aware system will require
works council approval, and rejection is a realistic outcome if privacy
safeguards are not convincing.

Source: internal (regulatory, labor relations).
Gives rise to: risk-works-council.
